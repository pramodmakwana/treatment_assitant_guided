# Liffey Clinic Protocol: Type 2 Diabetes in Adults

> Fictional protocol for a portfolio project. Not clinical guidance.

## T2D-1 Scope
Applies to adults (18+) with type 2 diabetes. Does not apply to type 1 diabetes, pregnancy, breastfeeding or children. For these, refer to the specialist team.

## T2D-2 Monitoring schedule
- HbA1c every 3 to 6 months until stable on unchanged treatment, then every 6 months.
- Kidney function (eGFR) and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) at least once a year.
- Blood pressure at every diabetes review.
- Lipid profile once a year.
- Diabetic eye screening once a year.
- Foot examination once a year.

## T2D-3 Glycaemic targets
Targets are individualised. For most adults, aim for an HbA1c of about 53 mmol/mol (7.0%). Use a less strict target for people who are frail, have limited life expectancy, or are at high risk of hypoglycaemia.

## T2D-4 First-line treatment
Offer metformin as first-line medicine unless it is contraindicated or not tolerated.
Metformin and kidney function: do not start metformin when eGFR is below 30. Review the need for, and the dose of, metformin when eGFR is between 30 and 45.

## T2D-5 Treatment intensification
If HbA1c remains above the individual target after 3 to 6 months, consider adding a second medicine.
- If the person has chronic kidney disease, heart failure or established cardiovascular disease, consider an SGLT2 inhibitor for kidney and heart protection, provided kidney function allows initiation according to the product information.
- Consider a GLP-1 receptor agonist where cardiovascular benefit or weight reduction is a priority.
- Sulfonylureas carry a risk of hypoglycaemia, especially in older people and people with reduced kidney function; use with caution.

## T2D-6 Hypoglycaemia risk
Review hypoglycaemia risk whenever treatment changes, especially with sulfonylureas or insulin, in older people, and in people with reduced kidney function.

## T2D-7 Referral
Refer to the diabetes specialist team if HbA1c stays well above target despite two or more medicines, if there are recurrent severe hypoglycaemic episodes, or if the diagnosis is uncertain.
